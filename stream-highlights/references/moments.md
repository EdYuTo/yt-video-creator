# What makes a stream moment work as a Short

A Short has to work for someone who has never seen the channel, with the sound on or off, and the first
second decides whether they stay.

## Patterns that worked (first run: a small CS2/Apex stream)
- **Self-own with a payoff**: "I'm top of the scoreboard, so we're doomed" → "the comeback starts now" →
  dies → round lost → laughs. Setup, promise, instant failure.
- **Friends roasting the streamer**: the streamer's own name used by friends as an insult ("playing like <name>"). The
  twist is understandable in one line.
- **Storytime with a punchline**: a story that builds for 30 s and lands on one quotable line. Cut
  the middle hard and keep the setup line and the landing.
- **Rage at the game**: "Where's my AK?" (the weapon vanished) → "trash game". It's short, relatable
  and gamers share it.
- **Small-streamer meta**: "2 subs, I'm famous" → "+1 follow… where are the mods?" → "nobody's
  watching". A montage of self-aware lines from across the VOD, which works because it's self-deprecating.
- **Brag after a fail**: dies bumping into a teammate, then claims he almost got a solo 5k.

## Also worth looking for
Clutches and aces (check the scoreboard or kill feed on a sheet), jump scares, chat
interactions ("chat, look at this"), strong opinions and hot takes, a bug or glitch, someone
walking in, the streamer laughing so hard they can't talk, a perfectly timed silence.

## Cutting
- **Start as late as possible.** The hook card carries the context ("SHOULD I RISK IT?"), so the footage
  can start at the good part. Drop greetings, "wait, wait" and dead air between lines. Jump cuts are
  normal on Shorts.
- **End on the laugh or the line, not after it.** The freeze-frame outro holds the punchline for 1.2–1.5 s
  and makes the loop feel intentional.
- Most good cuts are **20–45 s**. Anything past 50 s needs a second beat halfway through.
- The **hook text** states the tension, not the outcome: "TOP OF THE SCOREBOARD" (…of the losing team),
  not "I LOST".

## Ranking signals (find_moments.py) and their limits
Laughter (`kkk`, `haha`, `jaja`), swearing, surprise words, addressing chat, game-event words, story
setups ("so I…", "aí eu…"), speech density and loudness. They are good at finding where things
*happen*, and bad at judging whether it's *funny to a stranger*. Always decide by reading the transcript.
Chat logs (message rate, emote spam, clips), if the user can export them, are the strongest engagement
signal. Ask for them when the stream had a real audience.
